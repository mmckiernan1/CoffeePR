# Coffee Payroll Theme Standard

## Product feeling

Coffee Payroll should feel like a calm neighbourhood coffee shop that happens to make payroll easy: warm, familiar, uncluttered, trustworthy, and quietly capable.

The customer should never feel as though they have entered an enterprise payroll console.

## Core palette

| Role | Colour | Use |

| Dark roast | `#5A321F` | Coffee brand marks, secondary strong actions, anchors |
| Cream | `#FFFaf5` | Primary warm surface |
| Milk foam | `#FFFDF9` | Cards and raised surfaces |
| Latte | `#F7EFE6` | Page backgrounds and soft sections |
| Caramel | `#967663` | Eyebrows, supporting labels, warm metadata |
| Biscuit border | `#DECDBD` | Default warm borders |
| Cobalt | `#1557D8` | Primary action, current step, important navigation |
| Cobalt dark | `#0F47B5` | Primary hover/pressed state |
| Fresh green | `#5F7D4E` | Ready, successful, approved, completed |
| Green wash | `#EEF9E8` | Success backgrounds |

| Error red | `#DC2626` | True errors only |

## Usage rules

1. Warm neutrals create the environment. Cream, milk-foam white, latte and espresso should dominate the screen.
2. Cobalt is functional, not decorative. Use it for the next important action, the active payroll step and essential links.
3. Fresh green means success only. Use it for Ready, Approved, Complete and confirmed states.
4. Amber means attention, not failure. Use it when the owner needs to review something before continuing.
5. Avoid purple, magenta, teal gradients and generic fintech styling in customer-facing surfaces.
6. Avoid large cold-grey areas. Payroll should feel calm and human rather than institutional.
7. Rounded cards and generous spacing are preferred. Dense tables are reserved for operational detail and reports.
8. The coffee icon and Coffee Payroll name form the visible brand. Do not expose the former Comcheq name in customer-facing copy.
9. Internal identifiers may retain legacy names where changing them would create technical risk.
10. Plain language wins. Prefer “Pay employees”, “No time entry required”, “Here’s your payroll” and similar owner-facing wording over payroll-system jargon.

## Payroll flow

The six-step run is the visual centre of the product:

**Changes → Employees → Hours & pay → Review → Approve & pay → Done**

The user should always be able to see where they are, what requires attention, and what happens next.

## Visual hierarchy

- Page background: warm latte/cream
- Primary card: milk-foam white
- Current/next action: cobalt
- Brand anchor: espresso/dark roast
- Complete/ready: fresh green
- Needs review: warm amber
- Supporting copy: muted warm brown or neutral slate where readability is stronger

## Brand test

Before considering a screen finished, ask:

- Does this look like Coffee Payroll without reading the logo?
- Is there only one obvious next action?
- Does it feel warm and calm rather than technical and corporate?
- Are success and warning colours being used consistently?
- Is any old Comcheq, purple, magenta or teal styling still visible?
