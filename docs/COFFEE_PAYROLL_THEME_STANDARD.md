# Coffee Payroll Theme Standard

## Product feeling

Coffee Payroll should feel calm, clear, modern and reassuring. The name can carry warmth and personality; the interface should remain a professional payroll product rather than a literal coffee-shop theme.

The customer should never feel as though they have entered an enterprise payroll console, and the interface should not rely on brown, beige or café styling to communicate the brand.

## Core palette

| Role | Colour | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gun metal | `#1A2930` | Primary text and strong neutral contrast |
| Cobalt | `#1557D8` | Primary action, current step, important navigation and brand mark |
| Cobalt dark | `#0F47B5` | Primary hover/pressed state |
| Fresh green | `#5F7D4E` | Ready, successful, approved and completed states |
| Green wash | `#EEF9E8` | Success backgrounds |
| White | `#FFFFFF` | Cards and primary surfaces |
| Light neutral | `#F5F7FA` | Page backgrounds and quiet sections |
| Cool border | `#DDE6EB` | Default borders and dividers |
| Slate | `#647087` | Supporting labels and secondary copy |
| Cobalt wash | `#EDF3FF` | Current-step and informational emphasis |
| Amber wash | `#FFF8E7` | Review-needed and gentle warning states |
| Amber text | `#7A5D18` | Warning/review copy |
| Error red | `#DC2626` | True errors only |

## Usage rules

1. White and light neutral surfaces should dominate the application.
2. Cobalt is the primary functional and brand colour: use it for the next important action, current payroll step and essential links.
3. Fresh green means success only: Ready, Approved, Complete and confirmed states.
4. Amber means attention, not failure.
5. Avoid brown, beige, espresso, latte or coffee-shop surface palettes in the application UI.
6. Avoid purple, magenta, teal gradients and generic fintech styling.
7. Keep cards clean, rounded and generously spaced. Dense tables are reserved for operational detail and reports.
8. The coffee icon and Coffee Payroll name provide the personality; the colour system should stay crisp and professional.
9. Do not expose the former Comcheq name in customer-facing copy. Internal identifiers may retain legacy names where changing them would create technical risk.
10. Plain language wins. Prefer “Pay employees”, “No time entry required”, “Here’s your payroll” and similar owner-facing wording over payroll-system jargon.

## Payroll flow

The six-step run is the visual centre of the product:

**Changes → Employees → Hours & pay → Review → Approve & pay → Done**

The user should always be able to see where they are, what requires attention and what happens next.

## Visual hierarchy

- Page background: light neutral
- Primary card: white
- Current/next action: cobalt
- Brand anchor: cobalt with gun-metal text
- Complete/ready: fresh green
- Needs review: restrained amber
- Supporting copy: slate

## Brand test

Before considering a screen finished, ask:

- Does this look like Coffee Payroll without relying on brown or café décor?
- Is there only one obvious next action?
- Does it feel calm, modern and professional?
- Are success and warning colours being used consistently?
- Is any old Comcheq, purple, magenta, teal or brown-heavy styling still visible?
